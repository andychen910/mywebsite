THE STARTING POINT

A national market is still made of local decisions.

When borrowing becomes more expensive, housing demand may weaken. But prices do not necessarily adjust immediately, and the same national rate change may look different in California, Texas, or Ohio.

This project connects the timing question with the state comparison.

THE BOUNDARIES

Good analysis also explains what it cannot claim.

01

Association is not causation.

Without a stronger external identification strategy, the results will describe dynamic relationships rather than prove that every rate change caused a price change.

02

National rates are common across states.

The panel model studies differences through interaction terms, not fully independent state-level interest-rate shocks.

03

Quarterly data has limits.

The state analysis compares quarterly timing and magnitude, but cannot provide exact monthly response dates for every state.
